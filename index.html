Exercise summary

This exercise uses R and Plotly to make my Module 6 emissions chart interactive. It compares emissions from Brazil, China, India and the world from 1850 to 2024 based on the same saved dataset.

First of all, I checked whether there were missing values or duplicate country-year records in the data. Then, a line is created for each series and they are distinguished by colour and line pattern. Then, I added a hover label to show the specific year and its corresponding emissions value. Clickable legend entries allow readers to hide or display different series, while range sliders help users focus on shorter periods of time.

These interactive functions have their own uses: hovering displays precise values, legend entries reduce visual clutter, and sliders help readers analyse trends in the selected period of time. The vertical axis is kept between 0 and 25 tonnes per person to ensure that the axis scale remains consistent when the displayed time period changes.

In 2024, among the three selected countries, Brazil had the highest per capita emissions, reaching 13.2 tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalents, followed by China (9.9 tonnes) and India (2.9 tonnes). The global average is 6.7 tonnes per person. The above data includes land use emissions.

Title

Greenhouse gas emissions per person in Brazil, China, India and the world, 1850–2024
Summary

The interactive line chart compares the annual greenhouse gas emissions per capita for three countries and the world. The hover label shows the annual emissions value, the legend entry can control which series are visible, and the range slider is used to change the displayed period.

Mapping

The horizontal axis represents the year, from 1850 to 2024. The vertical axis represents the per capita carbon dioxide equivalent emissions (unit: tonnes) calculated on the 100-year warming timescale. These values come from the data extracted by the Our World in Data project, based on the research of Jones et al. (2025) and the population data processed by Our World in Data.

Brazil uses purple solid lines, China uses blue dashed lines, India uses brown dotted lines, and the world uses dark gray dash-dot lines. The legend identifies each curve. The World series represents the global average.

The hover label shows the year and emissions value, rounded to one decimal place. Click the legend entry to hide or display the corresponding series. The range slider is used to select the year range displayed in the main chart.
Important Notes

The saved Module 6 dataset contains 700 observations for four series from 1850 to 2024. No missing values or duplicate series-year records were found. Hover values are rounded to one decimal place, and the y-axis remains fixed at 0–25 tonnes per person when the year range changes. Emissions include land use and are estimates that may be revised. National averages conceal differences within countries, and the chart does not establish a causal relationship.
